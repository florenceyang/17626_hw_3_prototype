# Title - Legal Aid Intake Form

## Problem

People facing eviction or other legal problems apply for free help from a legal aid office through an online form, often on their phone and sometimes with a relative helping. The form is hard to understand, progress is easily lost, and applicants don't know if their request was received or when someone will call. Staff get incomplete answers and can't tell which cases are urgent, and some applicants are in unsafe situations where being found out could put them at risk.

## Requirements

1. Let applicants start and submit the form without an account.
2. Say up front that the service is free and who will see the applicant's information.
3. Provide a quick-exit button on every screen.
4. Ask the applicant what kind of problem they have and show only the questions that apply.
5. Ask about upcoming deadlines and danger before asking about household and income.
6. Show one question per screen and say what comes next.
7. Explain legal terms and required documents in plain language before asking the question.
8. Show how to reach a person for help on every screen.
9. Let applicants mark any answer as "I'm not sure."
10. Let applicants fill in the form alone or with a helper.
11. When a helper is present, ask the applicant to answer the danger questions privately, and don't show those answers again.
12. Ask for the date on the eviction notice and the court date as two separate questions.
13. Label court dates entered on the form as "reported by applicant, not verified."
14. Mark a request as urgent if the court date is within 7 days or the applicant reports danger.
15. Let applicants upload photos of their notice, lease, court papers and pay stubs, with instructions on how to take the photos.
16. Tell the applicant right away if an upload is unreadable or the wrong page.
17. Tell the applicant if a required document is missing, but still let them submit.
18. Let logged-in applicants save their progress and come back later.
19. Ask applicants to log in or create an account if they want to save progress or check their status.
20. Show logged-in applicants the status of their request ("Waiting for staff review").
21. After submitting, show a confirmation screen with a confirmation number, the documents received, and when to expect a call: same day if urgent, otherwise within 2 business days.
22. Never tell the applicant whether they qualify for help; every request waits for staff review.
23. Provide a simple staff list that shows each request's urgent flag, "not sure" answers, missing documents and unverified court dates.

## Other Notes

- Assumptions:
  - Document storage, accounts and the staff list are mocked, and the prototype uses mock data.
  - Court dates and other answers are not checked against court records.
  - Business days exclude weekends.
  - An "I'm not sure" answer to a danger question counts as danger reported.
- Unclear:
  - Requiring an account to save may be too much effort for some applicants, and login emails or texts could reveal that someone contacted legal aid. Both still need a decision from stakeholders.
  - Which documents are required for each problem type.
  - The exact wording of the danger questions.
  - What "same day" means for requests sent at night or on weekends.
- Out of scope:
  - Phone intake.
  - Eligibility decisions and income rules.
  - Conflict checks.
  - Assigning attorneys.
  - Staff callbacks and messages.
  - Verifying court dates.
  - Real document storage.
